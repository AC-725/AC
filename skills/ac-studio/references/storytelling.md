# Storytelling — the six devices, one story pass

**Added 2026-08-17.** AC flagged the six-device storytelling frame the big marketing pages
run on — hooks, emotional triggers, open loops, curiosity gaps, pattern interrupts, pacing
(the summary carousel: reputeforge, instagram.com/p/Dbp0bVtIGoh) — and asked for it built
into this brand. Three of the six were already law in pieces (hooks, arousal, the stake);
this file names all six, fills the gaps, and gates them with **one story pass** run on
every beat sheet. It is rebuilt on this page's own evidence base (hooks.md v2,
hook-research-2026-08.md), not imported as-is — the frame's usual implementation is
tease-and-withhold, and on an anti-hype page every device runs on a real, verified
number instead. Withholding is the one move this brand never makes.

**Division of law:** `hooks.md` owns the first three seconds and stays the deeper law for
device 1. This file owns devices 2–6 and the pass that checks all six together. Where a
rule already lives elsewhere (the render gates, the VO budget, the caption formula) this
file points at it, never forks it.

## The map — where each device does its work

| Device | News reel (A–E) | TOD (A–G) | Carousel (5 slides) | Caption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | scene A | scene A | slide 1 | line 1 (SEO phrase) |
| 2 Emotional trigger | chosen pre-write, felt by scene B | same | the proof beats (2–3) | the reframe |
| 3 Open loops | stake: A → the rule scene | stake: A → G | the sub line pulls each swipe | the question near the end |
| 4 Curiosity gap | the number vs the default assumption | tool vs old way | slide 1 → slide 2 | hook vs reframe |
| 5 Pattern interrupt | one break, scene C–D | the old-vs-now chart | slide 4, the turn | one short line after two long ones |
| 6 Pacing | onset every 2–4s; A fastest, close slowest | VO syllable budget | one breath per slide | line break every 1–2 sentences |

### 1 · Hooks — owned by `hooks.md`

The first device has its own file: six styles, stop/send grading, the blacklist, the
first-frame checklist. Nothing here overrides any of it. One law restated because the
other five devices depend on it: **the hook is the payoff, never a tease.** On this page
devices 2–6 exist to make a stated payoff hold for eleven seconds, not to compensate for
a withheld one.

### 2 · Emotional triggers — pick the feeling before writing

Information doesn't get sent; feelings do. Empathy out-shares every other emotion ~3×,
and high-arousal beats calm (already graded — hooks.md principle 7). The device is not
"add emotion", it is: **the feeling is chosen on the beat sheet before scene copy
exists, and produced by facts, never claimed by adjectives.**

The register this brand runs on:

| Feeling | Fires when | Reach for it on |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | the verified number breaks the default assumption | News default |
| Indignation | a price or claim doesn't survive checking — punch at companies, never at the viewer | receipts stories |
| Relief | "that was not you" / "there's a free window" | direct-callout, TOD |
| Awe | the capability jump is real and shown, not adjectived | TOD demo beats |
| Empathy / recognition | the viewer (or the friend they'll send it to) is named precisely | identity/Gift posts |
| Trust | calm receipts, named sources | every piece — the save emotion, the brand floor |

Banned registers: fear ("AI will replace you" is already blacklisted), mania, and
manufactured urgency. A dated-prediction hook runs on a real deadline — that is a fact,
not a register.

The make-it-better ladder, bottom to top:

1. **Stated** ("shocking", "insane") — hype texture. Never ships.
2. **Adjective-implied** ("a surprising number of…") — still telling, not producing.
3. **Produced** — two concrete facts whose gap makes the feeling arrive unprompted.
   "14 'free' tools. 6 were trials." No adjective anywhere; indignation shows up anyway.

Test: read the copy as a stranger. If the Arousal row's feeling hasn't arrived by scene
B, the copy is wrong, not the row.

### 3 · Open loops — everything opened gets paid

A loop is a debt: a question raised, held, then paid. Four loops, four different jobs:

- **The macro loop** is the stake (hooks.md rule 2): opened in scene A in the same
  breath as the payoff, closed at the rule scene. Exactly one per piece — a second
  macro loop splits the idea, and one idea per piece is older law than this file.
- **Micro loops** are the scene-to-scene debts: every scene ends owing the next one
  something. The working test — read the beats top to bottom and after each ask
  **"what is still owed?"** A scene that settles its own account is a dead end; this is
  what "scene B escalates or flips, never merely continues" is really checking.
- **The comment loop** is one small strand deliberately left open PAST the end: the
  caption question, the A/B choice card. It converts (comments → DMs); the send row
  reaches. Both live on the beat sheet, and they are different rows.
- **The replay loop** is the engineered seam (hooks.md rule 5): the last line is the
  first half of the opening sentence, the last frame matches frame 0, the audio bed
  runs unbroken across the cut.

The law that keeps loops on-brand: **never open a loop the piece can't close with a
verified fact.** Clickbait opens loops it can't pay; this page's edge is closing harder
than expected. The comment loop is the one legal open ending — and it stays open
because it's a choice offered to the viewer, not an answer withheld from them.

### 4 · Curiosity gaps — calibrated, never manufactured

The gap is the distance between what the viewer already believes and what the verified
number says. On this page the gap is **stated, not withheld**: "the tool nobody talks
about" is a withheld gap (blacklist-shaped); "14 'free' tools. 6 were trials." is a
stated one. Same psychology, opposite reputation cost.

The gap has a width, and both failure modes are real:

- **Too narrow** — the viewer already believes it, so there's no reason to stop.
  If the research didn't surprise you, it won't surprise them: sharpen the angle or
  drop the story.
- **Right** — the number contradicts the default assumption, AND the piece closes the
  gap with the mechanism (the *why*), not just the fact. Teaching the why is what turns
  a stop into a save.
- **Too wide** — true but unbelievable reads as hype and gets scrolled. The fix rides
  in the same breath: the credibility anchor (named source, named company) travels WITH
  the number. On a faceless page the citation is the tone of voice.

Beat-sheet row: write the viewer's **default assumption** in one line. If no default
assumption can be written, there is no gap; if there is no gap, there is no post.

### 5 · Pattern interrupts — break your own rhythm once, on purpose

Onsets keep the eye moving (gate 4); an interrupt resets the brain. An interrupt is a
**structural** change, not more motion. Three levels, all deliberate:

- **In-piece.** ONE scene mid-piece breaks the grammar the piece just taught: the
  toggle strike-through, a one-word scene after two dense ones, the chart after three
  text scenes, Rhythm's inverted gold slide, a carousel's turn slide. Default placement
  is scene C–D / slide 4 — the drop-off shoulder, after the open has settled and before
  the close. Gate 3 already enforces an architecture change in the scroll-decision
  window; this is the SECOND change, for the mid-tail, and it is written on the beat
  sheet, not discovered in the render.
- **In-feed.** The brand itself is the standing interrupt: flat black and one gold idea
  in a feed of faces and noise. Nothing to write per piece — everything to not break.
  The visual laws (no gradients, no stock, no grain) exist to protect exactly this.
- **In-series.** The page interrupts its own week: theme rotation, a Spine day, a light
  deck on occasion, the Saturday Prompt Drop. Check rotation in `run-log.md` so the
  interrupt is real — four crest days in a row means there is no interrupt left to spend.

Guardrail: **one in-piece interrupt.** Two is noise, and noise is the texture this page
exists against.

### 6 · Pacing — fastest at the open, slowest at the rule

The render side is already legislated, and this file only points: an onset every 2–4s
(hooks.md rule 3), nothing near-static past 1.0s (gate 4), cuts against budget (gate 5),
the VO syllable ceiling (beat sheet — scene A at the top of its band, the close the
slowest scene in the piece). What the device adds is the **writing side**:

- **Decelerate into the takeaway.** The densest verified fact lands early; the rule
  gets the most room. If the close is the fastest scene, the piece ends on a mumble.
- **One point per beat IS pacing.** A scene carrying two points is a pacing failure
  before it is a copy failure — split it or cut one (the beat sheet's own law,
  restated as rhythm).
- **Carousel: one breath per slide.** ≤3 words on the word slot; the sub line pulls the
  swipe. A slide the reader finishes with nothing owed is the exit slide.
- **Caption: the line break is the pacing device.** Every 1–2 sentences, one number,
  question near the end — the formula in `voice-and-caption.md`.
- **The dead-air test:** read the beat column aloud. Needing a second breath inside one
  beat means split or cut.

## The story pass — run on every beat sheet, before the checkpoint

Six rows, one honest line each. A row you have to talk your way through is a fail, and
a fail means **fix the story, not the wording** — a device that can't be filled in is
telling you the story is thin, the same way an unfillable send row is.

| # | Device | The check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | A- or better on stop AND B or better on send (hooks.md scale) |
| 2 | Emotion | The Arousal row names one feeling and the copy produces it cold, without adjectives |
| 3 | Open loops | Stake opens in scene A, closes at the rule; every scene leaves something owed; one comment loop survives the end |
| 4 | Curiosity gap | The default assumption is written down; the number contradicts it; the anchor rides with it |
| 5 | Interrupt | Exactly one in-piece break, placed mid-piece; series rotation checked against the log |
| 6 | Pacing | Fastest open, slowest rule; one point per beat; nothing fails the dead-air test |

The pass rides into the checkpoint beside the virality scorecard, and the scorecard's
watch-time row reads against rows 3–6. Grade honestly — and the validation loop from
hooks.md applies here in full: when a piece that passed the story pass underperforms one
that failed it, twice, recalibrate this file with a dated note rather than trusting it.
