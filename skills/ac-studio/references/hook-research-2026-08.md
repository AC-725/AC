## 1 · The one-line diagnosis

**Your hooks are graded on stopping power. Your zero-engagement problem lives on a different
axis: nothing is left open to resolve, and nothing names who should send it.**

The 2026-08-09 Insights drop said it plainly: reach fixed, but 562 views across 8 reels
returned 0 comments, 0 shares, 1 send. That is not a mystery — it is the *predicted* outcome
of the current design:

1. **Pure payoff-first closes every loop.** The thesis lands in scene A, the proof follows,
   the loop rhymes it shut. Nothing remains for the viewer to resolve, dispute, or complete —
   so there is nothing to comment on and no itch to pass along. (Zeigarnik-effect mechanics;
   Buffer's calibration rule: "Don't be so vague that you miss the window to trigger
   curiosity **or give away the full answer in your first line**.")
2. **The register is low-arousal.** The canonical share research (Berger & Milkman, *Journal
   of Marketing Research*) is unambiguous: high-arousal emotions — awe, indignation, anxiety,
   amusement — drive sharing; calm contentment suppresses it. A verified stat presented
   neutrally is credible and inert. Quiet-luxury *aesthetic* can stay; quiet *emotional
   register* is what caps sends.
3. **Practical value alone earns saves, not sends.** Digg's editorial data via Berger:
   utility "clicks well but doesn't always drive social sharing." Sends need identity — the
   sender forwards content *about the recipient* ("this is so you"). Of your three hook
   styles, only direct-callout is natively sendable.

None of this says abandon payoff-first. It says convert it: **payoff-as-new-question.**
State the thesis in scene A, and in the same breath attach the stake it creates. That is the
documented hybrid (Jenny Hoyos' foreshadow move: "$1 chicken sandwich. But is that enough to
beat Chick-fil-A?") that holds retention *and* leaves something open.

Two structural notes. First, Instagram shipped the tools to end grading-by-taste: per-reel
**Retention charts** and **Skip Rate** are live for professional accounts. The rubric's grade
is a prediction; Skip Rate is the result. From now on the run-log should record both, and the
rubric gets recalibrated whenever they disagree. Second, the "Your Algorithm" interest-editing
system reached the main feed in June 2026 — topic-coherent niche pages get matched more
cleanly, which favors your 90%-one-lane discipline.

across two independent Socialinsider samples (140K and 6M reels). But completion favors
ultra-short, and loops can beat 100% hold. So it is not "kill 11.7s" — it is **two deliberate

(30–45s, more beats — not stretched scenes). Choose per story. Today every News reel defaults
to the loop play without the loop being engineered hard enough to earn it (see §5).

## 3 · Why "A-grade" hooks under the current rubric underperform — and the regrades

As hook editor, I regraded the recent shipped hooks against the evidence. The current rubric
rewards clarity, distillation, specificity — all real. What it never asks: *what stays open
after the payoff lands, what does the viewer feel, and who sends it to whom.* Regrades below
use the v2 scale (stop-grade / send-grade).

| Day | Hook (scene A) | Old | v2 | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | "Your prompt failed. That was not you." | A | **A / send A-** | The best of the set, and the research explains why: empathy is the #1 share emotion (~3× anything else, Content Labs 4K-video dataset) and this is pure empathy + blame-lift. Keep making these. Send target was implicit; naming it ("send to the friend who gave up on ChatGPT") would finish it. |
| 28 | "14 'free' tools. 6 were trials." | A | **A / send B+** | Textbook receipts/proof-drop — the top save-generating archetype. Non-round numbers, checkable claim. Weak only on send: no named recipient. "Send this to whoever pays for the software" fixes it. |
| 27 | "You talk. The board moves." | A- | **B / send C** | Elegant, and it tests as elegant, not arresting. "The board" is ambiguous until scene B (already logged), no number in frame, no stake left open, nobody to send it to. The story had a stronger hook sitting in the research: the pricing contradiction. See rewrite below. |
| 26 | "Everyone is using AI. Almost nobody pays." | A | **A- / send B** | Real gap, two verified numbers behind it. But it states a closed verdict — the viewer nods and scrolls. One word of stake ("so why are you?") or an identity flip would open it. |
| 24 | "A $20 AI report. Yours for free." | A | **A- / send B+** | Price-flip structure — confirmed current (the "$14 tool replacing a $99 tool" pattern family). Good. Send is Idea-motive ("we should try this") but the copy never prompts the forward. |
| 20 | "Not the AI. The access." | A | **B+ / send C+** | Ouch, and worth being honest: **"It's not X. It's Y." is now on the AI-tell blacklist** — flagged across practitioner discourse as a ChatGPT-pattern phrase audiences pattern-match and skip. It was fine in August; it is burned now. The story itself was strong. |

**Rewrites, as worked examples** (each keeps the verified number, adds the missing axis):

- **Day 27 →** "Trello sells AI for $10 a month. It just gave the job away free." *(contrarian
  + pricing receipt + open question: what's the catch → scene F answers it. Send: Gift, "the
  friend who runs their business on Trello.")*
- **Day 26 →** "77% of owners use AI. Only 18% ever paid. The free tier is the market." *(same
  numbers, verdict now disputable — invites "PAID or FREE" instead of just presenting it.)*
- **Day 20 →** "Claude broke into three real companies. The AI did nothing wrong." *(concrete-image
  + contradiction carrying the stake; "did nothing wrong" is the open loop scene B closes.)*
- **Day 24 →** "The $20 research report Google forgot to paywall. Send it to whoever makes
  your slides." *(same payoff + named send in the caption/CTA layer, not on screen.)*

The pattern in all four: **the payoff stays first. A stake, a dispute, or a person is attached
in the same breath.** That is the whole v2 move on copy.

## 4 · The competitive field, and where you sit

The market is *exiting* the position the loud AI pages occupy — this is measured, not vibes:
hot-take hooks down 29% in 90 days; hook-first ad formats showing 22% steeper CTR decay
("we trained audiences to skip us" — Nik Sharma); "feels illegal to know" cloned to death;
AI-slop brand deals down ~30% (Collabstr via Business Insider); while **education/practical
Reels grew +31%** in the same period brand ER fell 23%. Calm-credible is the documented next
wave: date-stamped no-production finance channels growing on "data and conviction," Coin
Bureau's "the value is no longer information — it's interpretation, judgement and trust."

Anti-hype does NOT mean soft hooks, and this is the correction the page needs. The calm
creators who win run hooks that are *more* aggressive than the hype ones on exactly one
dimension: **specificity.** Ramit Sethi's formula is a quoted confession with an exact number
("We spend 179% of what we make"). Matt Wolfe's calm mode is curation-as-hook ("The ONLY
announcements that matter"). The four transferable anti-hype mechanics:

1. **Specific-number contradiction** — "We spend 179% of what we make."
2. **Calm contrarian reframe** — "Everyone says you need 10 AI tools. The data says one."
3. **Diagnostic** — "The reason your [task] isn't working."
4. **Curation-as-service** — "The only 3 worth your money."

Plus the one rising archetype in 2026: **dated prediction** ("On 18 Aug, Copilot loses deep
research. Here's your free window.") — predictions anchored to a real date/dollar are the only
hook class still climbing while everything else cools.

**Burned-phrase blacklist** (pattern-matched as noise in ~0.8s; never use): "Stop scrolling" ·
"feels illegal to know" · "I wish someone told me sooner" · "Don't walk, run" · "This will
change your life" · "Let that sink in" · "Here's the kicker" · "Plot twist" · **"It's not X.
It's Y."** · "99% of people…" · "If you're not doing X, you're losing money" · "You don't have
an X problem, you have a Y problem" · "AI will replace you / a person using AI will" (the Zain
Kahn line is 3+ years old and everywhere) · "Are you tired of…" · "Have you ever wondered…"
The meta-rule: **if a hook could appear on any AI page, it is already dead.** And the quiet
consolation from the same thread: "AI has made everyone sound the same, so being human is now
a superpower" — the em-dash ban and matched-source wording were ahead of this curve.

One more competitive finding worth stealing: on Instagram specifically, **visual + on-screen-text
hooks alone beat every stacked alternative** (34.7K median views vs 14.6K with spoken hook
added; Content Labs, 5,354 videos) — Reels is a silent, text-first surface. A typographic page
is not fighting a facelessness penalty in-feed; eye-tracking (U. Twente, TikTok feed) found
**text elements win first fixation over human faces**, and the faces penalty is mostly a
YouTube-thumbnail phenomenon. The format is an advantage. What is NOT an advantage is stillness —
next section.

## 5 · "Keen to look at" — the visual verdict

**Dark, calm, faceless is not the problem. Static is.** The evidence stack:

- The scroll decision happens by **~1.5s** (median scroll-stop on Meta Reels: 2.3s; retention
  gap between passing and failing reels is already 23 points at 1.5s — CreatorHouse, 1,000
  reels). The 3-second rule is where it's *measured*, not where it's *decided*.
- **Motion onset captures attention; continuous motion does not** (Abrams & Christ,
  *Psychological Science* — the classic finding, corroborated by banner eye-tracking). Ambient
  grain/drift will never stop a scroll; a thing that *starts* — a counter kicking, a line
  drawing, a toggle flipping — reliably does. Attention is bought by onsets, every 2–4s.
- Dark ground + one saturated accent is the *validated* salience architecture: the U. Twente
  eye-tracking winner was a desaturated scene with one high-contrast element ("you don't
  choose to look"). Black + one gold idea is structurally that design. In a 2026 feed trending
  loud and saturated, the one dark restrained object is the pattern interrupt.
- A **static mascot recovers none of the face deficit; an animated one recovers most of it**
  (static face ≈ no face; animated face ≈ 3.3× attention odds — Frontiers eye-tracking). And a
  figure's **gaze steers viewer attention to what it looks at**. The narrator-mode figure work
  from Day 29 is exactly the right investment; his gaze should always land on the number.
- 2026 premium motion language = "calm, premium motion replaces loud animations": restraint,
  physics-correct easing, one tactile texture, beat-locked micro-motion. Hype-bro = stacked
  glitch, shake, saturated yellow captions. The brand does not need to get louder. It needs
  more *onsets* per second of its existing language.

**The ranked visual upgrade list** (mapped to the engines; several are already law — noted):

1. **Frame 0 mid-motion, always.** Not just non-black and live (already law) — already
   *moving*: odometer mid-roll, gold rule mid-draw, headline mid-settle. A settled frame 0 is
   a static frame at the decision moment. *(News: wire scene A's odometer to start before 0.
   TOD: the typing bar types from frame 0 — good — but the headline should also be settling.)*
2. **Payload before 2.3s.** Hook line fully legible + the number started by 2.3s. News does
   this. TOD's claim currently completes around 3s after the Day 27 retime — pull the
   headline's final state under 2.3s.
3. **Engineer the loop as a seam, not a rhyme.** Current loop law is a copy echo. Upgrade to a
   true seam: **last line is the first half of the opening sentence** (the mid-sentence loop),
   last frame visually ≈ frame 0, audio bed continuous across the cut. Faceless formats hide
   seams best (no face-cue that a scene restarted). This is the cheapest watch-time doubler
   available to an 11.7s reel, and it is why the loop play stays viable against the length data.
4. **Scheduled onsets every 2–4s** — this is gate 4's real meaning, now with a design rule
   behind it: don't respace beats, add *starts*. News passes when copy gives every scene a
   moving element (Day 29 finding, confirmed); TOD's structural debt wants ambient large-area
   motion or full-frame beat changes — the research verdict is a slow push-in on the content
   block (3–8% scale over a scene) reads premium AND clears freezedetect. That is option 1
   from the Day 27 log, and the trend evidence removes the "changes the format's stillness"
   objection: slow drift IS the 2026 premium language.
5. **Keyword-highlight gold in the typed captions.** Chunked/word-by-word captions with ONE
   gold-highlighted keyword per line: +15–30% retention over static captions
   (operator-reported; the top-performing caption combination in creator testing). The
   subtitle engine already types — make the payoff word land first or hot, in gold, and the
   one-accent law becomes the retention mechanic itself. *(Both subtitle engines; small change.)*
6. **Second hook at 2–3s.** Hook and retention are separate problems (SaliencyLab: ads
   scoring 71 on hook, 38 on retention). Scene B's entrance is the contract renewal — it
   should escalate or flip, not continue. In copy terms: B answers the stake A opened, and
   opens the next one.
7. **One deliberate slow beat per reel.** After the fastest run, hold the key stat a full
   beat, micro-shimmer on the gold only. Contrast-as-emphasis is both a retention interrupt
   and the premium register. *(News scene D is the natural home.)*
8. **Non-round numbers on screen.** "$115.9B" over "$100B+", "650" over "hundreds", "17.7%"
   over "under 20%". Already house instinct; now it's a law with evidence.
9. **A tactile texture layer.** Subtle film grain or a specular sweep across the gold once
   per reel: 2026's anti-AI-polish signal ("flawless stopped working" — Canva's "Imperfect by
   Design"), and it masks the loop seam. Does NOT carry retention — onsets do (see #4).
10. **Cover ≠ frame 0, design both.** Feed autoplay judges the moving frame 0; the grid
    judges the poster. Already house practice; keep the 94px rule.
11. **Reading-speed floor:** captions ≤3 words/sec (~180 wpm ceiling), every text block
    readable ≥0.5s after its motion settles, ≤2 lines. The typed-caption engine is close;
    audit against the 0.5s-after-settle rule when copy runs long.
12. **Figure gaze = pointer.** Whenever the figure is on, he looks at the number. (Day 29f
    gesture map already does this — it is now a law, not a flourish.)

## 6 · What changes in the system (shipped in this pass)

**`references/hooks.md` rewritten to v2.** Payoff-first survives; everything else sharpened:

- **Scene A = payoff + stake in the same breath.** The Hoyos hybrid is now the house pattern.
  A hook that closes its own loop caps at B+.
- **Three hook styles become six:** thesis-first, concrete-image, direct-callout (kept) +
  **identity/Gift** ("AI tools for when you're the owner, the accountant, AND the marketing
  department"), **dated prediction** ("On 18 Aug…"), **receipts** ("14 'free' tools. 6 were
  trials.") — the save engine Day 28 discovered independently.
- **Grading gains three axes:** stake-left-open, arousal (what does the viewer *feel* —
  neutral caps at B+), and **sendability, graded separately** (named person + motive from
  Meta's five: Gift / Mirror / Idea / Gossip / Mood). Ship bar: A- stop-grade AND at least
  B send-grade, with the send named on the beat sheet.
- **Burned-phrase blacklist** added to anti-patterns (incl. "It's not X. It's Y.").
- **First-frame checklist v2:** mid-motion at frame 0, ≤7 words, stake by word 5, gold
  element large AND moving inside 500ms.
- **Validation loop:** grades get checked against per-reel Skip Rate and the retention chart
  when AC drops numbers; the rubric recalibrates when they disagree.
- **Length play** section: loop play vs depth play, chosen per story.

**`references/algorithm-2026.md`** gains a dated addendum (Aug 2026 numbers above) — the July
consensus it stated is intact, the benchmarks are now sharper. Scorecard unchanged at /10 but
its hook row now reads against v2 (which is stricter).

**`assets/beatsheet.template.md`**: share-trigger line upgraded to named person + motive;
stake and arousal lines added; hook-style list expanded to six.
