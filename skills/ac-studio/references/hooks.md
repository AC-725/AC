# Hooks & retention — read this first

A short reel is won or lost on two decisions: **does frame one stop the scroll, and
does the payoff come first or last.** Everything here maps to why a viewer stays or
swipes. On Instagram in 2026 the algorithm rewards **watch-time / completion** and,
more than ever, **sends/shares** ("sending to a friend" is a top signal) — so the
reel must hook in frame one and carry a "tag someone" idea.

## The five retention rules (in priority order)

1. **Open loud. Frame one is never black.** The most valuable half-second is the very
   first frame. A fade-from-black, a slow count from zero, or an unresolved tease all
   spend it saying nothing, and the viewer swipes. Frame one already shows the branded
   canvas AND the headline at full strength, hard cut in. Scene A is wired live on
   frame 0 for exactly this reason.

2. **Payoff first. Lead with the conclusion, justify with the proof.** The strongest
   line is the takeaway. Backloading it to second 8 means most viewers never reach it.
   Put the thesis in scene A and spend the rest earning it with numbers. Feed-native
   order is **answer, then proof** — never proof, then answer.

3. **One "moment" every ~2 seconds.** A moment is a visual event: a number odometering
   up, a toggle that strikes through, a chip that stamps in, a callback line. Each
   scene has one. If a scene is just a sentence with no motion, give it a number to
   count or fold it into its neighbor.

4. **Every claim carries a specific, real number.** "$725B", "95%", "280×" make the
   promise feel real. Vague superlatives ("the most powerful", "a genius move") do no
   concept-specific work and read as clickbait. Use the real figure, match the source's
   wording, back it with a named proof when you can. One big number per scene.

5. **Close on a loop, not a cold CTA.** End by echoing the opening line so the last
   frame rhymes with the first. It reinforces the one idea and nudges a rewatch, which
   the algorithm reads as strong retention. The follow prompt lives on the same card,
   quiet.

## The three hook styles (pick one per reel — this is Question 2 to AC)

Scene A always leads with the payoff; there are three proven ways to phrase it. Each is
shown with a generic example and an AI-news example (the news maps cleanly to all three).

1. **Thesis-first** — state the contrarian conclusion, the number proves it.
   `[Plain contrarian claim]. / [Who] just [did] [$number].`
   e.g. "Buying more AI won't save you." + $725B big-tech AI bill.
   News: "You don't need to build AI." + "Microsoft just bet $2.5B proving it."

2. **Concrete-image** — lead with the weird, specific, visual fact, then the shift.
   `[Who] is [surprising concrete action + number]. / Not [old thing]. [New thing].`
   e.g. "Your newest hire isn't human." + 79% of companies run AI agents.
   News: "Microsoft is dropping 6,000 people into normal businesses. / Not to build AI. To use it."

3. **Direct-callout** — name the viewer's mistake, then justify the correction.
   `If you're [common mistake], stop. / [Who] just [proof].`
   e.g. "If your AI plan is 'buy more,' you're wasting money."
   News: "If you're trying to build your own AI, stop. / Microsoft just bet $2.5B on using it."

All three: no em-dash, plain English, a real number, the claim is the payoff not a tease.

## Hook Machine grading principles (grade scene A against these)

Grade holistically, not by counting. Ship **A- or better**.

1. **Rapid context** — the topic is clear by the end of line one. The viewer can't opt
   into value they don't know is coming.
2. **Clarity** — zero ambiguity. If two people could read it two ways, rewrite. Silent
   #1 killer of hooks.
3. **Contrast / curiosity loop** — the gap between what the viewer believes and what
   you're claiming. Bigger gap on a topic they care about = more hooked. Can be stated
   or implied. An open loop ("the 5% do one thing") the brain needs closed is gold.
4. **Distillation** — fewest words possible. Cut any word you can lose without losing
   clarity or impact.
5. **Specificity** — numbers, names, timeframes. "95%" beats "most"; "one boring task"
   beats "the right work."
6. **Absorption** — processable at speaking speed on first listen. No jargon on a cold
   brain, no complex clause stacks.
7. **Instant value promise** — the hook contains what the viewer gets, not just a tease.
8. **Credibility anchor (bonus)** — a proof point (a company, a stat, a source) in the
   line or right under it. Not required, but rewarded when natural.

## Anti-patterns (auto-flag and fix)

- Fade / slow count from black on frame one → hard cut, headline already up.
- Payoff after ~4 seconds → move the thesis to scene A.
- Vague tease as the hook ("a truth nobody says", "this changes everything") → state
  the actual claim or the concrete surprising fact.
- A scene with no moment → add a number to count, or merge it.
- Two ideas fighting in one scene → one idea per scene; split or cut.
- Throat-clearing ("so basically", "I want to talk about") → delete.
- Jargon on a cold brain → plain language first.
- **Em-dash (—) anywhere on screen** → period, comma, or line break. It reads as
  AI-written. (Hard rule for on-screen copy.)

## Grade scale

- **A+/A** — loud frame one, payoff stated first, a real number as proof, zero
  ambiguity, nothing to cut.
- **A-** — strong on all of the above with one small fixable nit.
- **B** — right idea but leads with a tease, or the number is vague, or frame one is soft.
- **C or below** — no clear claim in scene A, or the payoff is backloaded. Do not ship.

## First-frame checklist (run on the t=0 QA frame every time)

1. Is the headline fully legible at frame 0 (not mid-fade)?
2. Is it the payoff, or a tease? (Must be the payoff.)
3. Could a stranger say what this reel is about from frame 0 alone?
4. Is the handle (whatever `HANDLE` is set to, e.g. `@itsac.ai`) on the frame, and is
   there exactly one gold idea?

If any answer is no, fix scene A before rendering the full video.
