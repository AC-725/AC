# Hooks & retention — read this first

**v2 · 2026-08-14.** Rewritten after the hook deep-research pass (see run-log entry and
`HOOK_RESEARCH_REPORT.md` delivered that day). What v1 got right survives: loud frame one,
payoff first, real numbers, no em-dash. What v2 adds, from evidence: the payoff now carries a
**stake**, hooks are graded on **arousal** and **sendability** (not just stopping power),
six hook styles instead of three, a burned-phrase blacklist, loop engineering, and a
validation loop against Instagram's own Skip Rate. Diagnosis that forced the rewrite: 8 reels,
562 views, 0 comments, 0 shares, 1 send — hooks that grade A on stop can grade F on send.

A short reel is won or lost on three decisions: **does frame one stop the scroll, does the
payoff come first, and does anything stay open after it lands.** On Instagram in 2026 the
algorithm rewards watch-time/completion and, above all for non-follower reach,
**sends per reach** (Mosseri-confirmed top-3 signal; comments are NOT top-3 — they matter for
DM conversion, not ranking). Roughly **63% of a brand reel's viewers skip inside 3 seconds** [UNVERIFIED, see note]
(Socialinsider, 140K business reels). That skip rate, not the rubric, is the scoreboard.

## The five retention rules (in priority order)

1. **Open loud, mid-motion. Frame one is never black AND never settled.** The scroll decision
   happens by ~1.5s; median scroll-stop is 2.3s. A settled frame is a static frame at the
   decision moment. Frame 0 shows the branded canvas, the headline, and something already
   MOVING — odometer mid-roll, gold rule mid-draw, headline mid-settle. Payload (hook line +
   the number started) fully legible by 2.3s. Scene A is wired live on frame 0 for exactly
   this reason.

2. **Payoff first, stake attached.** Lead with the conclusion — feed-native order is still
   answer, then proof. But a payoff that closes its own loop leaves nothing to resolve, and
   nothing-to-resolve is where comments and sends go to die. Scene A states the thesis AND
   opens the stake it creates, in the same breath: "X happened. But Y." / "X is true. So why
   are you doing Z?" The rest of the reel earns the payoff and closes the stake. (The Hoyos
   move: "$1 chicken sandwich. But is that enough to beat Chick-fil-A?")

3. **Something STARTS every 2–4 seconds.** Attention is captured by motion *onsets*, not by
   continuous motion (Abrams & Christ, *Psych Science*) — ambient drift never stops a thumb;
   a counter kicking, a toggle flipping, a line drawing reliably does. One onset per beat,
   a beat every 2–4s. This is also what gate 4 is really measuring: a News reel that fails it
   has a writing problem — add starts, don't respace. **Scene B is the second hook**: it
   escalates or flips, never merely continues (hook and retention are separate problems).

4. **Every claim carries a specific, real number — non-round beats round.** "$115.9B" beats
   "$100B+", "650" beats "hundreds", "17.7%" beats "under 20%". Vague superlatives do no
   concept-specific work and read as clickbait. Match the source's wording; the on-screen
   source name IS the tone of voice on a faceless page. One big number per scene.

5. **Close on an engineered loop, not a rhyme.** Replays count in Views and rewatch
   likelihood is an explicit ranking input. Upgrade the copy echo to a true seam: the last
   line is the FIRST HALF of the opening sentence (mid-sentence loop), the last frame
   visually matches frame 0, the audio bed runs unbroken across the cut. Faceless formats
   hide seams best — use it. The follow prompt lives on the same card, quiet.

## Who sends this, to whom (the send axis — new in v2)

Sends per reach is the growth lever, and a send needs a *recipient*. Meta's five sharing
motives: **Gift** ("this is so you"), **Mirror** ("this is so us"), **Idea** ("we should do
this"), **Gossip** ("did you see this"), **Mood**. Empathy is the #1 share emotion (~3×
anything else); trust drives saves; fear drives views but poisons an anti-hype brand.

Every reel names its send ON THE BEAT SHEET: a specific person-type + the motive. "Send this
to whoever pays for the software" (Gift) beats "share if you agree" (classifier-flagged
engagement bait). If the send row can't be filled with a real person the viewer would think
of, the story or the angle is wrong — fix that before writing scenes.

## The six hook styles (pick one per reel — this is Question 2 to AC)

Scene A always leads with the payoff+stake; six proven ways to phrase it:

1. **Thesis-first** — the contrarian conclusion, now with a disputable edge or open stake.
   `[Plain contrarian claim]. / [Who] just [did] [$number].`
   e.g. "Buying more AI won't save you. Microsoft just spent $115.9B proving it."

2. **Concrete-image** — the weird, specific, visual fact, then the shift.
   `[Who] is [surprising concrete action + number]. / Not [old thing]. [New thing].`
   e.g. "Microsoft is dropping 6,000 people into normal businesses. Not to build AI. To use it."

3. **Direct-callout** — name the viewer's situation or mistake, take the blame off them or
   correct it. The natively sendable style. e.g. "Your prompt failed. That was not you."

4. **Identity / Gift** *(new)* — name the person so precisely the viewer sends it to them
   (or IS them). `[Thing] for when you're [hyper-specific situation].`
   e.g. "AI tools for when you're the owner, the accountant, AND the marketing department."

5. **Dated prediction** *(new)* — the one hook class still climbing in 2026. Anchored to a
   real date or dollar, never vague futurism. `On [date], [specific change]. [Stake].`
   e.g. "On 18 Aug, Copilot loses deep research. Here's your free window."

6. **Receipts / proof-drop** *(new)* — the checkable claim, shown checked. The top
   save-generating archetype. `[N] [things]. [Surprising count] were [verdict].`
   e.g. "14 'free' tools. 6 were trials."

All six: no em-dash, plain English, a real number, the claim is the payoff not a tease, and
something stays open. Anti-hype calibration: calm ≠ soft. The winning credible hooks are MORE
aggressive than hype hooks on exactly one dimension — specificity.

## Hook Machine grading principles (grade scene A against these)

Grade holistically, not by counting. Ship **A- or better on stop AND B or better on send.**

1. **Rapid context** — topic clear by end of line one.
2. **Clarity** — zero ambiguity. If two people could read it two ways, rewrite. Silent #1
   killer of hooks.
3. **Contrast / curiosity loop** — the gap between what the viewer believes and what you're
   claiming, on a topic they care about.
4. **Stake left open** *(new)* — after the payoff lands, something remains to resolve,
   dispute, or complete. A hook that closes its own loop caps at B+.
5. **Distillation** — fewest words possible. ≤7 words on the first frame; stake inside the
   first 5 words of copy.
6. **Specificity** — numbers, names, timeframes; non-round beats round.
7. **Arousal** *(new)* — the stat is wrapped in a feeling: surprise, indignation, relief,
   awe, empathy. A neutral presentation of a true number caps at B+. (High-arousal drives
   sharing; calm contentment suppresses it — Berger & Milkman.)
8. **Absorption** — processable at speaking speed on first listen. No jargon on a cold brain.
9. **Instant value promise** — the hook contains what the viewer gets.
10. **Credibility anchor** — a named source/company/stat in the line or right under it.
    On this page the citation is the tone of voice.

**Send grade (separate, A–F):** who sends this, to whom, under which of the five motives?
Named person-type = B or better. Empathy or Gift framing = A range. "Nobody specific" = F,
and F does not ship.

## Anti-patterns (auto-flag and fix)

- Fade / slow count / SETTLED frame at t=0 → hard cut in, something already moving.
- Payoff after ~4 seconds → move the thesis to scene A.
- Payoff with no stake — the reel answers a question nobody is left holding → attach the
  "but", the dispute, or the person.
- Vague tease as the hook ("a truth nobody says") → state the actual claim.
- A scene with no onset → add a number to count, a line to draw, or merge it.
- Two ideas fighting in one scene → one idea per scene.
- Throat-clearing ("so basically") → delete.
- Jargon on a cold brain → plain language first.
- **Em-dash (—) anywhere on screen** → period, comma, or line break. (Hard rule.)
- Slow typed reveal that delays the stake past word 5 → type the payoff word first, then
  backfill.
- Like-begging (measured −4.9%) → ask for the comment, the save, or the send instead.

**Burned-phrase blacklist** (pattern-matched as AI/ad noise in under a second; never use):
"Stop scrolling" · "feels illegal to know" · "I wish someone told me sooner" · "Don't walk,
run" · "This will change your life" · "Let that sink in" · "Here's the kicker" · "Plot twist"
· **"It's not X. It's Y."** · "99% of people…" · "If you're not doing X, you're losing money"
· "You don't have an X problem, you have a Y problem" · "AI will replace you / a person using
AI will" · "Are you tired of…" · "Have you ever wondered…" · "Hot take:" without evidence.
The meta-rule: **if a hook could appear on any AI page, it is already dead.** (Day 20's
"Not the AI. The access." was strong in August and is blacklist-shaped now — the list ages;
re-check it when regrading old formulas.)

## Grade scale

- **A+/A** — mid-motion frame one, payoff+stake stated first, a real non-round number, zero
  ambiguity, a feeling attached, nothing to cut.
- **A-** — strong on all of the above with one small fixable nit.
- **B** — right idea but the loop closes itself, the number is vague, the register is
  neutral, or frame one is soft.
- **C or below** — no clear claim in scene A, or the payoff is backloaded. Do not ship.
- **Send grade travels with it**, e.g. "A / send B+ (Gift — the friend who runs on Trello)".
  Below B on send → rework the send row before shipping.

## The two length plays (choose per story)

Sub-30s is the WEAKEST reach band (5.20% vs 5.60% at 30–60s; sub-30s pulls less than half
the median views of 45–60s — two independent Socialinsider samples). But completion favors
short, and a true loop can post >100% hold. So:

- **Loop play** (sub-12s News): only when the seam is engineered (rule 5) and the story is
  one clean beat. The rewatch is the strategy.
- **Depth play** (~30–45s): the default direction for News when the story has 2+ numbers or
  a method. MORE BEATS, never stretched scenes — a stretched scene is a gate-4 failure and a
  dead tail. (A VO-retimed 24s cut is VO-only; it is not the depth play.)

TOD at 23.4s sits near the peak band; keep it.

## First-frame checklist (run on the t=0 QA frame every time)

1. Is something visibly MID-MOTION at frame 0 (not just present — moving)?
2. Is the headline legible, ≤7 words, payoff-side, stake inside the first 5 words?
3. Is the gold element large AND animate within the first 500ms (a gold number counting, a
   gold rule sweeping) — not a small static accent?
4. Could a stranger say what this reel is about from frame 0 alone, sound off?
5. Is the handle on the frame, exactly one gold idea, and does the copy avoid the blacklist?

If any answer is no, fix scene A before rendering the full video.

## Validation loop (new in v2 — the rubric is a prediction, not a result)

Instagram ships per-reel **Skip Rate** (% skipped inside 3s; brand average ~63% [UNVERIFIED]) and a
**Retention chart**. When AC drops numbers, log them next to the hook grade in `run-log.md`.
Targets: hook rate 50%+ (3s views ÷ impressions) · hold: sub-15s ≥50%, 30s+ ≥30% · a flatter
retention line beats a taller peak. **When an A-graded hook posts a worse skip rate than a
B-graded one twice, the rubric is wrong — recalibrate it here, in this file, with a dated
note.** Never ask AC for the numbers; grade honestly when they arrive.
