# Pacing, camera, type entrances, rule lines

## Pacing floor (AC, 2026-08-25)

11.7s across five beats read as "too fast to read." For a story with a comparison AND a catch,
budget **~2.8s per content beat** (six beats, ~15s). Every beat still needs its own motion; the
fix for "too static" was continuous camera work (zoom on the payoff, a hard angle cut inside
the opening scene, pull back for the comparison, push in for the catch), not faster cuts.

## Type entrances

Fades read cheap. Reveal headlines and rows behind a rising mask (`overflow:hidden` +
`translateY(104% → 0)`, easeOutCubic, 0.46s, 0.1s stagger per line). Keep frame-one elements
fully on at t=0 — the loud-open law outranks any entrance.

## Rule lines and source credits

AC, 2026-08-25: a rule line has to name the business stakes, not sound like a proverb. "Fast is
easy. Useful is hard." was rejected; **"Speed is solved. Work isn't."** shipped. Source credits
read better parallel across scenes (`THE 2026 GAMES · BEIJING · 22 AUG` /
`THE 2025 GAMES · WINNING TIME 21.50s`) than as one long event name. End card carries the CTA:
mark, handle, then `FOLLOW FOR DAILY AI`.

## Motion vocabulary · locked object (Day 41, 2026-09-11)

Drop + settle (ease-out-back) while the door shuts · quarter spin while the bolt slides ·
4% breathe at 1.8s once locked. Hook anchor: ±8° yaw quarter turn over the beat + 6px bob
at 1.4s. Nothing static past 1.0s (gate 4 still applies).
