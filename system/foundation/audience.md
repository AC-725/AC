# Audience — who this is for

Last updated: 2026-07-26 · confirmed by Austin

This file ends arguments about who a post is talking to. If a caption, hook, or offer drifts from the
people below, the content is wrong, not the audience.

## The one thing they share

**They are not technical, and they don't want to become technical.**

That single trait is what makes an otherwise broad audience coherent. It's also Austin's role in his
own words:

> "Making everything user friendly. And I am the connector for this."

He is the connector between ordinary people and AI that already exists. Not a builder, not a
researcher, not a hype merchant — the translator. Everything on the page should read as a connection
being made, never as a lecture.

## Who they are

Five overlapping groups, all confirmed. Written widest-first, because "general and beginner users" is
the outer ring the others sit inside.

| Group | What they're doing | What lands with them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General / beginner users** | Curious, starting from zero, unsure what's even real | Plain English, one idea, no assumed knowledge |
| **Small business owners** (non-technical) | Run the place, decide the spend, drowning in admin | Hours returned, not features |
| **Solo operators / freelancers** | One-person business, buying back time, looking bigger | Do-it-today tools, leverage without headcount |
| **Ops / office managers in SMEs** | Not the owner — the person actually doing the manual work | Fix for the specific painful task; they recommend upward |
| **Aspiring AI consultants** | Want to do what Austin does | Proof, method, "here's how I'd approach it" |

Geography: **Hong Kong → worldwide** (carried from `ac-instagram`; the content is not HK-specific).

## What they want

All four confirmed, in rough order of pull:

1. **Get hours back on boring work.** Quotes, invoices, scheduling, admin. They want their evenings
   back — the technology is incidental. Lead with the hours, never the tool's cleverness.
2. **Not get left behind.** Low-grade anxiety. They keep hearing AI matters and want to know what's
   real without being sold to. This is why anti-hype *is* the value, not just a style choice.
3. **Concrete tools they can use today.** Show-me-the-thing. The daily tool or trend they can try
   before they close the app.
4. **Eventually hire Austin.** The page qualifies buyers as well as teaching. Never in-feed — the CTA
   ladder keeps the offer in Highlights and DMs.

What they already believe, and what has to move: *"AI is for tech people / it's overhyped / I'm too
late."* Each post should leave one of those slightly less true.

## Who it is NOT for

Confirmed exclusions. These do more work than the inclusions.

- **Engineers and AI builders.** They want to build models and write code. "Don't build, just use" is
  the exact opposite of what they're looking for, and writing for them breaks the promise.
- **Hype-chasers and crypto-adjacent.** They want "this changes everything." They inflate reach, never
  convert, and chasing them destroys the anti-hype positioning that is the whole differentiator.

**Deliberately not excluded** (so nobody later assumes they were):

- *Enterprise / big tech* — not a target, and `ac-instagram` positions against "tech giants," but
  Austin did not name them as a hard exclusion. Treat as "not who we write for," not "turn away."
- *Free-only tool collectors* — fine as reach. Don't aim content at them, don't refuse them either.

## Open — do not invent

- **What they'll pay for, and at what price.** Unresolved, and it belongs with the Offer pillar, which
  is still missing. Do not infer a price or a willingness-to-pay from this file.
- **Where they are besides Instagram.** No confirmed data on other platforms or what else they consume.

## The tension worth watching

**Small business owners and aspiring AI consultants are different buyers.** "Get your evenings back"
and "here's how to do what I do" pull in different directions — different pain, different proof,
different offer. Both are confirmed, so this isn't an error to fix; it's a split to hold deliberately.

Practical rule until Austin decides otherwise: **write for the owner, and let the aspiring consultant
learn by watching the method.** One piece of content, two readings. If a post can only work for one
group, make it the owner — that's the larger ring and the clearer buyer.
