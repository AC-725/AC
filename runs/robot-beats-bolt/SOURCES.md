## The caveat that matters — no ratification

No independent body ratified the 9.39. Bolt's 9.58 was ratified by World Athletics; the
federation sanctioning the robot games grew out of the event itself, so the time was
effectively measured by the same industry that built the robot.

This is the strongest material on the page's "no hype" positioning and nobody else is
running it. It is in the caption and it is the whole idea behind cover concept D.

## The number that did NOT survive verification — 21.50

The first-draft caption leaned on "last year the winning robot ran 21.50, so it is twelve
seconds faster in one year." That comparison does not hold:

- At the 2025 inaugural games, **Tien Kung Ultra** (same maker, earlier robot) won the
  100 m with **21.50 s**.
- But the 2025 games applied a **time coefficient**: a remotely controlled run scored at
  1.0, a **fully autonomous run at 0.8**. Tien Kung Ultra physically finished **third**
  and was declared the winner once the coefficient was applied.
- So 21.50 is an **adjusted** figure, while 9.39 is a **raw heat time**. Putting them side
  by side and calling it "twelve seconds faster" overstates the gap.

**Handling:** the "12.11 seconds faster" line was cut from cover concept B and replaced
with a neutral "One year later" plus an asterisk footnote. The claim was dropped from the
caption entirely rather than caveated, because a cover cannot carry that much footnote.
