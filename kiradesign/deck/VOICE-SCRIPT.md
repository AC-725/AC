# KIRA Design — Pitch Script

**For:** Kim Wong, Design Director
**Deck:** `KIRA-Design-Website-Revamp.pptx` / `.pdf` (5 slides)
**Runtime:** ~6–7 minutes talking, before questions
**Goal of this meeting:** not to get final sign-off — to get Kim to say "start on it." Everything below is built toward that one ask on slide 5.

Stage directions are in *[italic brackets]*. Everything else is close to what to actually say — not a script to read verbatim, a script to talk from.

---

## Before you open the laptop

Say this first, before slide 1 is even up:

> "I want to show you five slides, and I want to be upfront — this isn't a proposal I'm asking you to approve today. It's a direction. I want your gut reaction, and if it lands, I want to start next week."

*[This does two things: it lowers the stakes so Kim reacts honestly instead of politely, and it names the ask before you've earned it — so it doesn't feel sprung on him at the end.]*

---

## Slide 1 — 紅牆 · The Red Wall

*[Open here. Let it sit on screen for a second before you say anything. Don't explain the concept yet — let the red and the oak do the work.]*

> "So — this is the direction. Before I tell you what it is, just look at it for a second."

*[Pause. Actually pause — 3 full seconds feels long but it isn't.]*

> "This palette isn't something I invented. It's your reception. The red wall, the black lettering, the oak battens behind you every time you sit down with a client — that's the whole site now. Nothing here is a stock 'luxury interior' template. It's built out of the room you already sit in."

*[If you're on the real site rather than the deck for this bit, this is a good moment to switch tabs and let the hero section breathe on a big screen. Otherwise stay on the slide.]*

> "Design, build, furnish — the three companies — that line stays on screen through the whole homepage. That's deliberate. I'll come back to why."

---

## Slide 2 — The work is better than the website

> "Here's the actual problem, and I want to say this plainly: your work is genuinely better than what's representing it online right now."

*[Walk the four findings quickly — don't dwell on each one, they're meant to land as a fast list, not four separate arguments:]*

> "Four things, specifically. The awards sit on top of your own photography and cover the rooms they're meant to prove. Projects read as thumbnails, not evidence — no developer, no area, no scope, so a buyer can't actually assess you from the page. Henderson, Chinachem, Wheelock, Grand Ming, Poly, K&K — the strongest thing you have — is sitting in small grey text in brackets. And the category labels don't even match the grid underneath them."

*[Now point at the red card and let him read it. Don't paraphrase it first — just point.]*

> "But this is the sentence that actually matters. This is yours."

*[Pause while he reads it.]*

> "'Minimal, but it does not bring out the Interior Design advantage.' That's the whole brief, in your own words. Restraint only reads as confidence when something is being withheld — right now the site is just quiet, not confident."

---

## Slide 3 — Your palette was already in your reception

> "So here's the idea, and it's simpler than it looks. I didn't design a new identity for you. I looked at the one you already have."

*[Walk the swatches left to right, don't rush this — this is the emotional center of the pitch.]*

> "Lacquer red. Black. Oak. And this—" *[point at the orange chip]* "—is the same orange as the I in your own wordmark. It was already there. It just wasn't on the website."

> "Almost nobody in Hong Kong luxury interiors uses red. It's auspicious, and more practically — it means nobody can mistake this site for a competitor's. That's not something a template can give you."

---

## Slide 4 — Built, not sketched

> "And this isn't a mockup — this is the actual homepage, running."

*[If you have a screen and connection: switch to the live site right now. Click the EN / 繁體 toggle and let the whole page flip languages in front of him. This single gesture is worth more than the rest of the deck combined — do not skip it if you can help it.]*

> "Bilingual, English and Traditional Chinese, one toggle. Responsive on mobile. And on the right—"

*[Back to the slide if you switched away, walk the five fixes fast:]*

> "—every problem from two slides ago, solved. Awards move into their own honours rail, off the photography. Projects become full plates — developer, area, scope, year, award, every time. Your six developers get their own section. Four clear routes instead of two mismatched labels. And the enquiry form itself routes — a developer brief and a private homeowner reach different people on your team automatically."

---

## Slide 5 — Three things, and it ships

*[This is the close. Slow down here — this is the ask.]*

> "Here's what I need from you to finish it. Three things, and none of them are complicated."

*[Walk the three cards:]*

> "Your photography — whatever exists, whatever quality it's in. Every image slot on the site is already sized and labelled for exactly what it needs, so there's no guessing on my end. The hotel projects — names, locations, years, scope — and Hospitality becomes a real fourth category instead of a placeholder. And the logo as an SVG file, so I can get the letterforms exact instead of approximating them."

> "Once those three things land, I build the individual project pages, the three sector routes, and get the Chinese copy read by someone in your office before it's final. Two weeks from there to a site you can actually send a developer."

*[Now the actual ask — say it directly, don't soften it into a question that's easy to defer:]*

> "So — can we start this week? I don't need all three things today. I need a yes, and then the photography whenever you can get it to me."

*[Stop talking. Let him answer. Don't fill the silence.]*

---

## If he pushes back

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course — but can I ask what specifically you want to think about? Is it the direction, or is it timing?" *[This separates a real objection from a stall. If it's the red — go back to slide 3 and the reception photo. If it's timing — ask what timeline would work instead of dropping it.]*

**"Can we keep it more minimal / less red?"**
> "Sure — tell me where it feels like too much. My worry with less red is we're back to slide two's problem: quiet without being confident. But I'd rather tune it than lose you on it."

**"What does this cost?"**
*[Have your number ready before you walk in — don't discover it live in the room.]*

**"Send me the deck and I'll look at it properly."**
> "Happy to — it's already yours." *[Send the PDF right there if you can, from your phone. Then:]* "One thing I'd still love before I go — even a rough guess on the hotel projects, so I'm not blocked on that piece."

---

## After the meeting, same day

Send a two-line follow-up regardless of how it went — this is what actually starts the two-week clock:

> "Thanks for the time today. To recap: [whatever he agreed to]. I'll get started on [X] — the one open item on my side is [photography / hotel list / logo file], whenever you're able to send it."

Get *something* in writing today, even informal. A verbal "let's do it" in a meeting evaporates by Thursday if nothing follows it in text.
